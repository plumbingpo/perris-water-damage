Wet Carpet Drying: What Perris Homeowners Should Know

There is always more water in a soaked carpet than the surface suggests. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. Towels and a shop vacuum reach the top layer and little else. In Perris, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Whether the carpet can be saved depends on two things, how clean the water was and how long it has been wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Under the wet section, the floor gives a little or squeaks when you step on it
  • Loose, wavy sections where the tack strip no longer holds the carpet
  • Backing that separates from the carpet when you lift a corner to look
  • Carpet that still feels cold and clammy to bare feet days after the spill
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped

How We Fix It

How We Handle Wet Carpet Drying

1

Map the Wet Area

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry All the Layers

Depending on the job, we float the carpet on moving air or lift it and remove the pad. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reinstall

Where needed, an antimicrobial goes on the subfloor and carpet, then new pad is laid if the old was removed, and the carpet is stretched back on its tack strip and cleaned.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Spreads Sideways

The pad wicks water well past the visible wet spot. The tack strip starts taking on water as it creeps below the walls toward the room next door.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

At this stage, removing both carpet and pad is usually the only option. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Water Held in the Pad

Pad can hold many times its weight in water. It keeps feeding moisture into the carpet above and the floor below long after the surface feels dry.

Damp Base of the Walls

Water that runs under the carpet reaches the base of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Swollen Subfloor

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. Squeaks, soft spots and raised seams show up weeks after the spill is forgotten.

Stains From Furniture

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.
