Of all the problems an owner can have, sewage coming up through the drains ranks among the most dangerous. Pathogens in raw sewage include salmonella, giardia, hepatitis and E. coli, along with other viruses, parasites and bacteria, and contact with anything contaminated is a serious risk to health. Across Perris, Perris Restoration answers sewage emergencies 24/7, sending trained technicians who arrive with EPA-registered disinfectants and the right protective equipment so your property is decontaminated safely.

The industry classifies sewage as black water, or Category 3, which is the most dangerous and most contaminated type of water damage there is. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. We work to the IICRC S500 standard that governs Category 3 losses, with the goal of returning your property to a condition safe to occupy.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. We also deodorize the affected area with industrial-grade hydroxyl generators and thermal fogging equipment to eliminate lingering sewage odors.

Signs It Is Time for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Running a sink or washer and hearing a toilet or nearby drain gurgle
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Drain openings or basement slabs coated with visible sewage residue
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • A rotten egg or sewer smell from the drains or the lowest floor of the home
  • Soggy, smelly ground over the sewer line or leakage at the cleanout cap
  • Dark, dirty water or sewage rising out of a tub, toilet or floor drain

Why Hire a Professional for Sewage Cleanup?

Sewage cleanup is not something any homeowner should attempt. Because raw sewage is a biological hazard, the work demands trained people, protective equipment (gloves, boots, respirators and Tyvek suits) and disinfection products registered with the EPA and not sold to the public. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Disposal matters as well, since everything the sewage contaminated has to be handled as biohazard waste. It is illegal to throw sewage-contaminated insulation, drywall or carpet in the regular trash, and doing so puts the community and the workers who collect it at risk. A certified restoration company follows proper disposal protocols and works with licensed facilities that accept biohazard waste.

Sewage Cleanup Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Keep cooking oil, grease and food scraps out of the drain, where they build into clogs
  • A drain that is slowing down should be dealt with early, while it is still partly open
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Use drain strainers in showers and sinks to catch hair and debris
  • Toilet paper is the only thing to flush, and that rules out wipes sold as flushable, which still clog
  • A backwater valve at the sewer connection stops the city system from backing up into your home

What each item is made of decides that. Glass, sealed tile, metal and other hard surfaces that do not absorb water are normally salvageable after disinfection. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. For insurance purposes, our crew keeps a list of everything that had to be removed.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Grease buildup, wipes and other items that should not be flushed, and tree roots growing into the pipe are the usual causes. Sometimes an aging pipe cracks or caves in, or a storm overwhelms the city sewer and forces sewage up the floor drains. A camera run through the line after cleanup identifies the actual cause.
Standard homeowner's insurance often does NOT cover sewage backup unless you have a specific sewage or water backup endorsement. Adding that endorsement is something we strongly recommend: it typically costs little, about $50 to $100 a year, and the protection is critical. Look at your policy documents, or call your agent to confirm.
Removing the sewage and decontaminating the property is usually done in 1 to 3 days. How extensive the damage is determines the rest, and restoring everything with new materials may need 1 to 3 weeks. Our first priority is making the property safe to occupy again as fast as we can.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Any water sent down a drain adds to the problem, so hold off on toilets, taps and draining appliances.
